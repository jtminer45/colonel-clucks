About us

Who we are

Colonel Clucks Poultry is a small, farm-raised agric start-up in Jos, Plateau State. Batch 1 is 50 broilers, which means every order gets a personal reply from us and not a form letter.

We are growing in public. That means we share what we do each day, the numbers we keep and the things we are still learning, on this page and on our Instagram, Facebook and X pages. If you are planning your Christmas table, our pre-order page and size guide will help.

Routine

A day on the farm

Broilers grow fast and they grow while they eat, so we check them four times a day and top up feed and water late at night. Most of our work is looking, listening and keeping things clean.

Our daily checks
TimeCheckWhat we do
5:30amMorning check (the most important)Take a torch, because it is still dark until about 6:30. Look before touching anything: healthy birds are active, spread out and noisy. Remove any dead birds and write down the number. Note whether feed or water ran out overnight. Wash and refill the drinkers. Top up feed so feeders are about two-thirds full. Keep the sides mostly closed until the chill lifts.
12:00pmMidday checkTop up water and feed. Birds drink most in the warm afternoon. Turn wet or caked litter, especially around drinkers. Sniff the air: a strong ammonia smell means we need more airflow or drier litter.
4:00pmAfternoon checkTop up water and feed. Walk slowly through the flock and watch for limping, sleepy birds, runny droppings or a bird sitting alone. Film a short clip for our social pages.
6:30pmEvening lock-downFill the feeders to the top for the night. Fill the drinkers. Close the sides against the cold Jos night but leave a small gap at the top for air. Switch on the light, with about an hour of dark. Check doors against dogs, cats and rats.
7:30pmRecordsLog the day: deaths, feed used, water used and how the flock looks.
9:00 to 10:00pmLate top-upA quick feed and water top-up so nothing runs dry before morning.

Nutrition

What the birds eat and drink

Broilers move through three feeds as they grow: starter, grower and finisher. We change feeds gradually, mixing the new feed in over about three days. We never ration. If the feeders run empty, we give more.

Growth

How we track growth

Every Monday we weigh 5 random birds and log the average. That tells us early if the flock is falling behind. If it is more than about 0.2 kg behind, we check feed, water and cold nights first.

Housing

Housing and comfort

  • Covered house. Our birds live in a covered house with ventilation we adjust through the day and night. We do not describe them as free range.
  • Cool Jos nights. We close the sides at night but always leave a small gap at the top so fresh air can get in.
  • Light. The light stays on overnight with about an hour of dark.
  • Dry litter. We turn wet litter every day and replace soaked litter completely. Wet litter and ammonia are early warning signs of trouble.
  • Clean water. Drinkers are washed and refilled every morning, and deep-cleaned every Friday along with the feeders.
  • Security. Doors are checked every evening against dogs, cats and rats.

Health

Health and records

We write everything down. Each day we log deaths, feed used, water used and whether the flock looks healthy, and we keep a health calendar for vitamins and any vaccines or treatments. Records are how we spot a problem on day one and not day five.

What we do not claim

We have not applied for organic or other certification labels, so we do not use those words about our birds. What we can show you is our daily routine, our records and photos and video from the farm.
